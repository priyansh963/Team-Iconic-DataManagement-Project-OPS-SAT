What the FAIR assessment showed

Score of the original dataset: 6 / 8 (11.5 / 15 in detail)

	Score	What it means
F Findable	2	Easy to find: DOI, keywords, indexed on Zenodo and OpenAIRE
A Accessible	2	Easy to get: open download, preserved by Zenodo
I Interoperable	1	Hard to understand correctly: misleading label column, unexplained channel codes, no unit for value
R Reusable	1	Hard to reuse safely: two licences, no codebook, a mistake in the paper (1,494 vs 1,594)

The key conclusion:

The data is easy to find and download, but someone using it straight away could easily make mistakes.

For example, someone training a model on the label column would learn nothing, because it says “anomaly” on every row.

What this means for your group’s question (“Can this data be trusted and fed into an AI model?”): not directly as downloaded. It’s trustworthy at the source (real ESA data, peer-reviewed, checksums match), but it needs documentation and cleaning first. That’s exactly what the rest of the project does.

How FAIR connects to the other parts

FAIR is the starting point: it says where the problems are. The other roles fix them:

FAIR gap	Who fixes it
Misleading label, zeros, irregular sampling	Profiling measures them, then Tableau Prep and the Pipeline clean them
No codebook, missing units	Governance: the data dictionary
Unclear history of changes	Governance: lineage; Pipeline: the run log and checksums
Two licences	You: the licence statement (done)
